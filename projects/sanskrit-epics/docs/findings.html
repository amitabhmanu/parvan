What Parvan Caught

Taking each scored claim in the dating of the Sanskrit epics back to the critical editions turned up three rows the manuscripts contradict — one rated 5 of 5. It also turned up something larger: a way to test whether a dating method can tell late from early at all, and the answer for one whole class of evidence is that it cannot.

The result

Both brackets are now separable: strip out every constraint resting on scholarly assertion and neither moves. That is something nobody could say about these dates before, because the evidence and the interpretation had never been held apart and counted.

The whole network

Everything above comes out of one graph, and this is all of it — 85 nodes and 124 constraints, nothing pruned for the picture. Eight of those constraints carry both headline brackets. They are the ones drawn in rust.

How a date actually reaches a text. The three columns are not decoration; they are the direction of inference, and each arrow type does one job.

  • A material horizon grounds a concept. Iron metallurgy did not exist in South Asia before about 1300 BCE, so nothing that presupposes iron weaponry is older than that. This is the only kind of edge that can supply a floor, and only excavation can make the claim — letting a text make it would let the network date itself.
  • A dated object attests a concept. A coin, an inscription, a translation catalogue. Attestation caps a concept from above and never bounds it below: the Heliodorus pillar proves Vāsudeva worship existed by 110 BCE and says nothing about how much earlier it began.
  • A text presupposes a concept and inherits its floor. This is the step that turns archaeology into a date for a poem, and it is where almost all the network's mass sits — 34 of the 124 edges.
  • A text that should mention a concept and does not floors that concept instead. Powerful, and the most dangerous edge in the store: it needs a positive control before it counts, which is why the Pāṭaliputra silence earns one and the Rāmāyaṇa's silence about liberation does not.

Follow the rust and the argument is bare. The Rāmāyaṇa core's floor is three links — a Kosala power horizon, the concept it grounds, the stratum that presupposes it. Its ceiling is five, and passes through the Mahābhārata on the way. Every one of those chains is a minimum cut of 1: cut any single link and the bracket moves.

A network of 124 constraints in which 8 do all the work is not a redundant network. It is a thin argument drawn at scale — and drawing it at scale is what makes the thinness visible.

Three claims the manuscripts contradict

Scored floors that the text does not bear out · two rated 4 of 5, one rated 5 of 5

Dīnāra, the Roman denarius not in the text Rāmāyaṇa floor · scored 5 / 5

One of only four score-5 floors in the entire Rāmāyaṇa inventory, and the basis for placing certain passages after 100 CE. The word does not occur.

d[iī]n+[aā]ra Baroda critical edition 0 / 18,761 verses southern recension file 0 Hellwig revision, kāṇḍas 2-3 0

Zero under every spelling, diacritic-folded included, across all three files. The likely explanation is that the claim rests on vulgate passages the Baroda editors excised — which would make it a floor on material outside the constituted text, in an inventory that elsewhere presupposes the critical edition.

Ayodhyā as a great fortified metropolis wrong stratum Rāmāyaṇa floor · scored 4 / 5

Credited to the core, and used to floor it at c. 600 BCE. The canonical description belongs to Bālakāṇḍa — the book the same inventory classes as a later stratum, whose lateness nobody disputes.

kapāṭatoraṇavatīṃ … uccāṭṭāladhvajavatīṃ śataghnīśatasaṃkulām … durgagambhīraparikhāṃ durgām anyair durāsadām Rām.1.005.010–013 — gates and archways · high watchtowers and banners · thronged with hundreds of śataghnī · a deep impassable moat · a fortress unassailable by others

Book 2 — Ayodhyā's own book, inside the core — has zero śataghnī and zero gopura. Its one moat-and-rampart passage describes the army's road camps on Bharata's march; another describes Rājagṛha. The core's entire fortification datum is the single word aṭṭālakeṣu in a list of city features, which does not support the claim.

Greek-derived astrological vocabulary not in the text Mahābhārata floor · scored 4 / 5

The floor placing the didactic layers after c. 200 CE. None of the three loanwords the source names occurs anywhere in the epic — archetypal text or apparatus, under any spelling.

horā 363 hits → every one is ghorā "terrible", ahorātra "day-and-night", kaṭhorā "harsh" \bhorā 3 hits → all elided ahorātra kendra 4 hits → sandhi artefacts: janaka+indra, baka+indra drekkāṇa 0 hits → zero under every spelling tried

Worth dwelling on the shape of that. A term occurring zero times returns 363 raw hits — a keyword count alone would have confirmed the claim handsomely. Only reading them shows every one is an ordinary Sanskrit word.

The fair statement: the scored row names no specific terms, and these three come from an earlier section offering them as Greek loans into Sanskrit jyotiṣa generally — a claim about the language, not about this epic. So the row cannot be checked as written, and the obvious candidates fail.

Pāṭaliputra absent as imperial capital holds — and beats its score ceiling for both epics · scored 2 / 5

The lowest-scored row in either inventory turns out to carry the pre-Common-Era ceiling for both epics. Zero hits across 123,372 Mahābhārata verses and all 18,761 of the Rāmāyaṇa, under four names the city is known by, plain and diacritic-folded.

What lifts it above an ordinary argument from silence is a positive control:

tena ruddhā hi rājānaḥ sarve jitvā girivraje MBh.2.013.062 — by him all the kings, once conquered, are held at Girivraja · Jarāsaṃdha's capital, named repeatedly; and Rājagṛha again in the tīrtha catalogue

The epic's Magadhan geography is not undescribed. It is described, and stops one city short. Silence from a text that names the predecessor capital is far stronger evidence than silence from a text that names none — a distinction a single 1-to-5 score cannot express, and the reason this row's rating is the least reliable number in either table.

Hūṇas in the peoples lists holds · archetypal Mahābhārata floor · scored 5 / 5

The source calls this the latest binding floor on the epic, so whether it stands on constituted text or on material the critical editors excluded is the difference between a real floor and a dīnāra. It stands: every occurrence is archetypal, none in the apparatus.

cīnān hūṇāñ śakān oḍrān parvatāntaravāsinaḥ MBh.2.047.019 — Cīnas, Hūṇas, Śakas, Oḍras, dwellers between the mountains. Also MBh.6.010.064, MBh.12.312.015
Massed elephant corps, fourfold army holds · institution, not animal Mahābhārata floor · scored 4 / 5

Elephants appear constantly and mostly irrelevantly — as a city epithet (gajasāhvaya = Hastināpura), as ivory, as tribute, as one named sixty-year-old tusker. A floor at c. 500 BCE needs the military institution, not the animal, and the institution is defined outright:

rathasyāsan daśa gajā gajasya daśa vājinaḥ … akṣauhiṇīti paryāyair MBh.5.152.019–022 — ten elephants to a chariot, ten horses to an elephant … which by these names is called an akṣauhiṇī

And in use at MBh.6.058.031: anīkaṃ daśasāhasraṃ kuñjarāṇāṃ, a ten-thousand-strong elephant division engaged as a single tactical unit.

Books 1 and 7 divinize Rāma; books 2–6 do not holds · differential intact Rāmāyaṇa stratification · scored 5 / 5

The load-bearing argument that the core predates its frame, and the highest-rated internal evidence in the Rāmāyaṇa inventory. It holds — once the divine-assembly retrofit at 6.105 is carved out of the core, which the source already identifies as a seam.

mānuṣīṃ tanum āsthāya rāvaṇaṃ jahi saṃyuge … viṣṇur ātmavān pitaraṃ rocayām āsa tadā daśarathaṃ nṛpam Rām.1.015.003–007 — "taking a human body, kill Rāvaṇa in battle" … Viṣṇu resolved on Daśaratha as his father. Rāma's birth narrated as an incarnation, in narrator voice, before the character exists. Cf. Rām.7.008.025 bhavān nārāyaṇo devaś caturbāhuḥ sanātanaḥ

The core, minus the seam, yields 25 viṣṇu and 4 nārāyaṇa hits across 14,065 verses — and not one is a narrator- or deity-voiced identity claim. They are similes ("strong as Viṣṇu", grammatically a comparison between two distinct beings), background myth, Rāma worshipping Nārāyaṇa as a separate god, and two character speculations, the second of which the text retracts five verses later.

An unexpected detail. Rāma's own ātmānaṃ mānuṣaṃ manye — "I consider myself a man" — occurs exactly once in all 18,761 verses, at 6.105.010, one verse before Brahmā answers it. The disclaimer and the declaration are not opposing evidence from different layers. They are two beats of a single scene, both inside the carved-out seam.

Karma, rebirth and mokṣa as an assumed framework holds in one epic, partly in the other floor in both · scored 4 / 5

One row, three components, and the two epics do not support them equally.

The Mahābhārata has all three explicitly and repeatedly in archetypal text — tyaktvā dehaṃ punarjanma naiti (Gītā 4.9), and even Brahmā's realm described as punarāvartin, subject to return.

The Rāmāyaṇa core has karma binding across births — kīdṛśaṃ tu mayā pāpaṃ purā dehāntare kṛtam, Sītā asking what sin she committed in a former body — and no trace of liberation at all:

Rāmāyaṇa core, 14,065 verses: punarjanman 0 saṃsāra 0 apunarbhava 0 mumukṣu 0 mokṣ 53 hits → releasing a necklace · releasing wealth as gifts shedding tears · loosing anger at enemies not one soteriological

The referent was split in two. Worth setting beside the grammar row below: this bundle could be repaired and that one could not, because these components are separately checkable and those are not.

The eḍūka polemic holds · the gloss does not Mahābhārata floor · scored 4 / 5

The passage exists, is archetypal, and is unambiguously Kali Yuga material. The floor at c. 250 BCE, however, depends on identifying eḍūka as a Buddhist reliquary mound — and the passage names no Buddhist.

MBh.3.188 bauddha 0 śramaṇa 0 nagnaka 0 buddh 4 → every one of them buddhi, "mind"

What it says is eḍūkān pūjayiṣyanti varjayiṣyanti devatāḥ — "they will worship eḍūkas and abandon the gods" — inside an undifferentiated list of degeneracy signs, beside śūdras teaching dharma and untimely rain. The anti-Buddhist reading is an identification brought to the text rather than found in it, so the floor rests on that identification and not on the verse.

What citation-level checking can and cannot reach

Two claims resisted settlement in entirely different ways, and telling those ways apart is the most portable thing here: one lies outside what any concordance can see and always will, while the other was perfectly measurable and turned out, once controlled, to be measuring something else.

Post-Vedic, non-Pāṇinian grammar five of six components attested floor in both · scored 5 / 5

The source calls this "the tightest single bracket" available and rates it the strongest evidence in either inventory. It bundles six typologically distinct changes. A concordance over the two epics now reaches five of them — and the one it cannot reach turns out to be unreachable for a reason no amount of data fixes.

Reachable. Absolutive chaining is pervasive and quotable — 1,298 verses of the Rāmāyaṇa core carry a word-final -tvā, with three-absolutive chains findable in both epics. -tum is the only surviving infinitive: the older -dhyai, -tave and -tavai return zero genuine survivals once tava+eva sandhi is stripped out. And the lost subjunctive is now measured — see below.

Not reachable, and not for want of data. Absent pitch accent looks like a transcription problem: GRETIL's stated default is that "accents have been dropped in order to facilitate word search." But it makes an explicit exception, and ships the Śaunaka Atharvaveda fully marked — yé triṣsaptā́ḥ pariyánti víśvā rūpā́ṇi bíbhrataḥ — at the same pāda-level addressing as the epics. The obstacle is the other side of the comparison. Accent notation is a Vedic scribal apparatus, and no epic manuscript in any recension carries it. The epic's unaccented state records how the text was transmitted, not how it was spoken — and no edition, however good, can supply a mark the tradition never wrote. This is the third appearance of one pattern: an absence that may record what kind of text it is rather than when it was written, alongside the Rāmāyaṇa's silence about liberation and the compound-length result below.

The subjunctive, and why the obvious search fails. Counting word-final -āti gives 24.7 per 10,000 words in the Atharvaveda against 7.0 and 8.2 in the epics — an apparent 3.5× enrichment that is entirely noise: root-class and class-9 presents (yāti, jānāti, gṛhṇāti) and sandhi artifacts (nāti = na+ati), all of which the Vedic text carries too. The subjunctive of a thematic present instead lengthens the stem's own -a, so the count has to be paired within stem — bhavati against bhavāti — which eliminates every confounding form by construction.

28 thematic stems, three person slots: Atharvaveda 85,489 words 200 ind 46 subj 18.7% Rāmāyaṇa core 160,993 words 247 ind 0 subj 0.0% Mahābhārata 903,578 words 1,778 ind 0 subj 0.0% expected at the Vedic rate: 57 and 409

And it has the positive control the mokṣa silence lacked. The subjunctive did not vanish outright — its first person survived by being reassigned to the imperative, and the same search finds it: karavāṇi ×39 and dadāni ×32 in the Mahābhārata. The category is visible to the instrument; the second and third persons are gone from the text. Being morphology rather than topic, it carries no genre confound, so this one was encoded rather than withheld.

The two that looked hardest. Aspectual collapse and past-participle-as-predicate are claims about how forms function across a whole text, which no single locus settles — so the same move works: express the function as a ratio against its own alternative. The perfect displacing the imperfect as plain narrative past runs 0.08 in the Atharvaveda against 0.60 and 0.88 in the epics; the instrumental-agent participle construction runs 6.2 per 10,000 words against 14 to 22. Both encoded.

And one metric that looked best of all, refuted. The aorist falls from 4.33 per 10,000 words in the Atharvaveda to 0.19 in the Rāmāyaṇa — until a control text runs it. Kālidāsa, c. 400 CE, comes in at 4.89, the Vedic rate and twenty-five times the Rāmāyaṇa's. A metric on which a fifth-century poet resembles the Atharvaveda is measuring register, not date. Recorded as unusable rather than encoded — the same confound that sank the compound-length measurement.

A compound presupposition is only as strong as its weakest component, and the weakest here is not weakly evidenced — it is unmeasurable on one side by the nature of the transmission. The highest-rated evidence class in both inventories is the one least amenable to citation-level checking, and knowing precisely which of its six parts are blocked by cost, which by method, and which permanently is worth more than the aggregate score of 5.

A later section revisits this row and does not leave it standing. Attesting five of its six components turned out not to make them do anything: a preregistered falsifier fired on exactly this class, and the ablation is below.

The Rāmāyaṇa's silence about liberation measured, and refuted would have tightened the headline by 200 years

The silence documented above is real and measured, and encoding it would have moved the Rāmāyaṇa core's ceiling from 300 BCE to 500 BCE — the largest single move available anywhere in the network. It was withheld pending a control. The control has now been run, and it kills the argument.

The decisive control was not the external one. It was the Rāmāyaṇa's own late books.

soteriological vocabulary, per 10,000 words Rām 2-6 (core) 160,993 words 0 hits 0.00 Rām 1 (Bāla) 21,711 words 0 hits 0.00 Rām 7 (Uttara) 31,111 words 0 hits 0.00 Mahābhārata 903,578 words 88 hits 0.97 Raghuvaṃśa 18,418 words 3 hits 1.63

Books 1 and 7 are zero too. They are later than the core by this network's own attested constraints, and they demonstrably absorbed the avatāra theology the core does not carry — the differential two cards above. If the silence tracked date, the late books would have broken it. They do not.

Meanwhile the genre plainly permits the vocabulary: the Mahābhārata uses it, and so does a court epic on the same dynasty written around 400 CE. The whole Rāmāyaṇa runs zero at every stratum, early and late alike. The silence belongs to the tradition, not to the date, and it bounds nothing.

This is a better outcome than promotion would have been. A 200-year tightening bought on a silence that turns out to be traditional would have been the largest error the network could contain — and it would have looked like the best result in it.

A measured silence is not automatically evidence of date. Before it counts, something has to rule out its being evidence of genre.

What a second text actually buys

Corpus growth buys checking, not resolution — and the exchange rate is measurable. Adding an entire second epic to the network, 25 nodes and 40 edges, moved exactly one shared referent, and that one binds nothing.

The mechanism is worth having explicitly. A referent's bound is set by its earliest attester, so a second source moves it only when it supplies a new extreme. Precision therefore scales logarithmically in corpus size while independent cross-checks scale linearly — which is worth knowing before anyone spends a year digitising in the hope of tightening a date. The return on more text is real, and it arrives as confidence rather than as precision.

The apparatus check

The Mahābhārata's electronic text preserves the critical edition's apparatus; the Rāmāyaṇa's does not. Of 224,679 lines, 66,177 are star or appendix passages — material the BORI editors judged non-archetypal.

That makes one question askable for the first time: does this floor rest on constituted text, or on an interpolation? It is the question dīnāra raises and cannot answer, because the Rāmāyaṇa has no apparatus to consult. Every Mahābhārata claim above was run twice, with and without it.

Two instruments nobody had used

Everything to this point was an argument somebody had already made, checked against the text. This is the reverse: read the corpora, find signals the scored inventories never proposed, test them. Two came out — and the second one refutes a whole class of evidence this network was relying on.

The critical apparatus is a labelled lateness set calibrated · then it discovers 66,177 lines · 28% of the transmitted text

The Mahābhārata's editors marked, passage by passage and on manuscript evidence, which lines they judged non-archetypal. That is a per-passage label of relative lateness in the same work, the same genre, the same language and the same scribal tradition. Every external comparison this project makes — Vedic against epic, epic against Kālidāsa — varies genre, register, subject and centuries at once. This one varies date and almost nothing else.

Dīnāra, the Roman coin and the inventory's clearest late marker, occurs zero times in the constituted text and only in the apparatus. So does pustaka, "book". Early material culture is flat. The instrument works, so it can be turned around and asked what it finds on its own.

Ranking all vocabulary by log-odds rather than testing a list produces an unprompted and completely coherent signature: umā, maheśvaraḥ, namaḥ, puṇyaphalaṃ, dānaṃ, karmavipākena, śṛṇu — Śaiva and goddess devotion, merit-and-gift ritual, and didactic framing, accreting onto a constituted text whose own markers are vaiśaṃpāyana, rājan, raṇe, iva: narrative, martial, courtly. That is what the scholarship says qualitatively. Here it is measured, and it was found rather than assumed.

Grammar fails its own test no discriminative power the method class rated 5 / 5

Run this network's own linguistic dating metrics — the ones attested three cards above — against that label.

metric archetypal apparatus shift perfect/imperfect ratio 0.88 0.94 1.07× PPP-predicate per 10k 14.90 14.26 0.96× aorist per 10k 1.12 0.93 0.83× thematic subjunctives 0 0 — mean word length 7.01 7.07 1.01×

Every one is flat. Lexical content moves by fifteenfold and more across exactly the same boundary; grammar does not move at all.

The reason is not mysterious once stated: an interpolator adding verses to the Mahābhārata writes epic Sanskrit on purpose. The register is a target, not a symptom. So linguistic stratigraphy does what it was shown to do earlier — separate Vedic from epic, a real gap of centuries and a change of genre — and cannot separate epic from epic-plus-centuries.

The score-5 grammar row therefore survives as exactly what it claimed to be, a floor. What does not survive is the hope of using features of that class for fine-grained stratification: on the one labelled dataset that exists, they have no measured power at all, and referential content has plenty. This is the third independent arrival at one confound — register imitated rather than inherited — after compound length and the aorist.

The writing horizon encoded · the first backstop a ceiling nobody in the inventory proposed

The Rāmāyaṇa core does not merely lack writing. It visibly does something else in the slot a literate culture fills with a document, which turns an argument from silence into an argument from substitution.

The root is present and pre-literate. √likh occurs eleven times in the core and never means "write". Ten are one formula:

śṛṅgair ambaraṃ vilikhann iva  ·  kailāsaśikharākāro dṛśyate kham ivollikhan Rām.4.040.028, Rām.6.030.023 — peaks and palaces scraping the sky. The word for "write" is sitting there in its original sense of scratching, doing no literate work at all.

The Mahābhārata archetype has moved one step and no further — √likh has reached draw: citre 'pi ca likhaty aśvān, "he draws horses in a picture". And pustaka appears in the apparatus and nowhere else in the corpus. One root, three stages: scratch → draw → books.

The control is the plot itself. The core has 25 dūta episodes and 18 uses of abhijñāna, "recognition token" — nine times the Mahābhārata's rate. Its central problem is the one a letter exists to solve: prove a messenger genuine to a captive behind enemy lines. It is solved with a signet ring.

dūto rāmasya … rāmanāmāṅkitaṃ cedaṃ paśya devy aṅgulīyakam Rām.5.034.002 — "I am Rāma's messenger; look at this ring marked with Rāma's name." Sītā answers not with a reply but with her crest jewel. Objects bearing a personal name exist; writing does not.

And the genre excuse does not apply: later layers of the same tradition acquire the vocabulary, which is precisely where pustaka turns up.

What it buys is insurance, not precision. Encoded at the late end of the Brāhmī debate so the bound is as weak as the evidence allows, it is slack by 70 years and tightens nothing. But this network's worst structural weakness has been that the pre-Common-Era ceiling for both epics rests on one row scored 2 of 5, with a minimum cut of 1. Ablation:

scenario Rām core ceiling as built 300 BCE (via Pāṭaliputra) Pāṭaliputra attestation removed, writing kept 230 BCE (via Aśokan Brāhmī) both removed 250 CE

If the Pāṭaliputra argument falls, the ceiling now slips 70 years instead of 480.

The apparatus was sitting in the files the whole time, labelled by the editors, and it answers a question the scored inventories cannot: not whether an argument is believed, but whether its instrument can tell late from early at all.

An independent tradition says the same thing

Every text checked so far belongs to one transmission. The Pāli canon does not: separate language, separate manuscripts, separate scholarly apparatus, and no stake whatever in Sanskrit epic chronology. It was added to test the two claims that matter most — and it replicates both.

Dīgha-Nikāya, text only lipi / lekhā as script 0 potthaka (book) 0 kahāpaṇa (coin) 0 √likh 2

Both √likh occurrences are one stock phrase — saṅkha-likhitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, the holy life "polished like a conch-shell". That is the same scratch-and-polish sense the Rāmāyaṇa core uses eleven times and never leaves, in a tradition with no transmission in common with it.

And its control is of a better kind than the Rāmāyaṇa's. The Brahmajāla and Sāmaññaphala suttas enumerate the low arts a recluse abstains from — dozens of items — and the list runs:

muddā, gaṇanā, saṃkhānaṃ, kāveyyaṃ, lokāyataṃ DN i.11, i.69 — finger-reckoning, counting, computation, poetry, Lokāyata. It names the skills that sit next to writing and stops short of writing itself.

A narrative may omit anything. A list that aims at completeness over its domain and omits an item is a far stronger silence — the enumerative control this project has been short of since the mokṣa row died for want of one.

Pāṭaliputra, corroborated from inside the transition the warrant, independently checked the row carrying both epic ceilings

The whole pre-Common-Era ceiling for both epics rests on one argument from silence: the epics name Girivraja as Magadha's capital and never Pāṭaliputra, and a text listing capitals would name the current one. The Pāli canon shows the same distribution — Rājagaha is the operative capital on 22 pages, Pāṭaliputta appears on 2 — and dates the change from inside it. Within two pages the same place is a village, then a city not yet built:

Sunīdha-Vassakārā Magadha-mahāmattā Pāṭaligāme nagaraṃ māpenti Vajjīnaṃ paṭibāhāya DN ii.86 — Magadha's chief ministers building a fort at the village of Pāṭaligāma. Two pages later, DN ii.88: Pāṭaliputtassa kho Ānanda tayo antarāyā bhavissanti, the three dangers foretold for Pāṭaliputta.

Deliberately not encoded as a constraint. Everything this text can attest about that referent is slacker than the Mauryan horizon already supplies, so an edge would add a node and move nothing. It is recorded as evidence for the warrant, because what it changes is confidence rather than arithmetic — and corroboration is not redundancy: a second argument for the same conclusion, running through the same terminus, does not make the network any harder to break.

What did move, and the direction is the surprise. The Dīgha-Nikāya was entered at [400 BCE, 100 BCE], deliberately late at the ceiling so its own absence claim would be as weak as possible. The solve returns [400 BCE, 230 BCE]: the Pāli collection's ceiling pulled back 130 years by its own ignorance of writing, propagating through the Aśokan attestation. This is the first corpus addition in the whole project to tighten anything — and what it tightened was not an epic but the newcomer itself.

A network built to date Sanskrit epics dated a Pāli collection instead, and the epic brackets did not move by a year. That is the law this project keeps re-deriving: more text buys checking, and checking is not the same as knowing more.

What was actually carrying the ceiling

Every bound in this network ends at a material horizon that nothing inside the network dates. Pulling those three nodes apart turned out to be the most informative thing done to the store all week — and it explains why no amount of new text was ever going to make it more robust.

One node was two dating chains in a trench coat precision borrowed, not earned the node carrying both epic ceilings

The horizon that caps both epics read, in full: "Pāṭaliputra excavation; Nanda accession through early Mauryan consolidation" — site stratigraphy and dynastic chronology, in one interval, labelled archaeological-horizon. Separated into the chain anchored in Seleucid history (Strabo XV.1.36, Megasthenes at Palibothra) and the chain anchored in the Kumrahar excavation reports, then ablated one at a time:

scenario Rām core ceiling as built 300 BCE archaeological chain removed 300 BCE (no change at all) historical chain removed 230 BCE

Archaeology alone supports nothing tighter than 150 BCE. The entire 300 BCE figure came from the Greek historical chain, while the node presented it under an archaeological label. That is this store's known weakness — category references standing in for citations — caught in the one place it mattered most.

The independence is also only partial, and is now recorded as such: the Kumrahar timber palisade is conventionally identified as the wall Megasthenes describes, so the site's phasing leans on the very account it is supposed to corroborate.

Why redundancy cannot be bought structural, not fixable every cut still 1

Three independently transmitted corpora have now been added and not one raised a minimum cut. The reason is not that the sources are weak. It is arithmetic:

A cut counts the routes that realise a bound. Two independent chains raise it only when their precisions match — and real evidence almost never comes in matched pairs.

Two honest chains reaching the same conclusion with different tightness give one binding route and one slack one: cut 1, permanently. The measure is telling the truth about the evidence, not about the store. What extra evidence does buy is measurable — backstop distance, how far a bound falls when its binding route is removed. For the Rāmāyaṇa's ceiling that is now 70 years, against a 550-year collapse a week ago.

One tradition, two collections, and the scribe appears in one the genre excuse, closed Vinaya · 1,511 PTS pages

The strongest objection to the writing horizon was always that a heroic narrative might simply have no occasion for documents. The Pāli canon answers it from the inside, because it contains both kinds of text in one language and one transmission.

Dīgha-Nikāya Vinaya lipi (script) 0 0 lekhaka (scribe) 0 yes kahāpaṇa / māsaka / rūpiya 0 / 0 / 0 7 / 18 / 16

And the decisive passage is enumerative, which is why it was worth going for. Vin iv.8, a rule against insulting speech, lists the trades:

… naḷakāraṃ kumbhakāraṃ pesakāraṃ cammakāraṃ nahāpitaṃ … muddikaṃ gaṇakaṃ lekhakaṃ Vin iv.8 — basket-maker, potter, weaver, leather-worker, barber as low; reckoner, calculator, scribe as high. That is the same slot the Dīgha-Nikāya fills with muddā, gaṇanā, saṃkhānaṃ and no scribe.

Two readings were withdrawn on inspection, both of which would have overstated the case: potthaka at Vin i.306 is a coarse cloth in a list of forbidden robes, not a book, and avalekhanakaṭṭha is a latrine scraping-stick, not a stylus — √likh in its old sense, sitting in the same volume as the scribe.

Encoding the Vinaya changed no epic bracket, as every addition has. But the network now entails an ordering it was never built to produce: the Dīgha-Nikāya must precede writing's emergence and the Vinaya attests it, so DN ≤ writing ≤ Vinaya. A machine assembled to date Sanskrit epics has issued a falsifiable claim about the relative order of two Pāli collections — exactly the kind of output it exists to generate, and exactly the kind a Pāli scholar can shoot down.

A prediction made in advance, and what happened to it

Before any of this network existed — before there was a solver to run — four falsifiers were written down and frozen. They are stopping conditions, not tuning opportunities: each names a result that, if it came out a particular way, obliges the project to say so. The first one has now fired.

F-1 · Metrical evidence carries nothing fired frozen before a solver existed
"If ablating every metrical-statistics edge does not degrade anchor recovery, then metrical arguments carry no usable information, and the source document's claim that language and metre form the tightest single bracket is wrong. Report it as such." preregistration.md, F-1 — written in Phase 1

The specified test could not answer it, and that had to be said first. Asked a different way — remove the class and see what moves anywhere in the network:

Every bound that moves is a referent that exists only to carry this class of argument. Not one text stratum moves. Rāmāyaṇa core and Mahābhārata core are unchanged to the year, and so is every other work-state in the store.

The metrical and linguistic edges form a closed pocket. They bound the concepts they themselves introduce and transmit nothing to any text. Remove them and the only thing that loses its date is the argument itself.

They are not weak evidence for dating the epics. They are not evidence for dating the epics at all — a self-contained description of the language, correctly recorded, wired to nothing. Three independent routes now agree: the ablation moves no text, the apparatus test shows no discriminative power, and the specified instrument cannot see anything either way.

What follows, because the preregistration requires it rather than permits it. The source's highest-rated evidence class supplies no bracket on either epic. The row survives only as a floor — post-Vedic, resting on a comparison with the Atharvaveda slack by 450 and 700 years. And the next phase of this project, which proposed to infer strata from vipulā ratios, compound length and morphological frequencies in sliding windows, is retired before it was run: it would have spent weeks inferring layers from features now measured three ways to carry nothing.

The fourteen edges stay in the store. They are accurate records of an argument the field makes, and deleting them would hide the finding rather than state it.

What is new here, and what is not

Most of what is above restates things Indologists have long held, now measured. A smaller part has, as far as could be established, not been done before. It is worth separating the two explicitly, because a project whose whole argument is about the difference between evidence and assertion cannot be vague about its own.

Method — no precedent found

  • A critical apparatus used as a labelled lateness set. The Mahābhārata's editors marked 66,177 lines non-archetypal on manuscript evidence — a per-passage label of relative date, holding work, genre, language and transmission constant. Treating that discarded apparatus as ground truth for validating dating metrics turned up no precedent when searched for; the nearest related work in manuscript dating deliberately avoids using palaeographic estimates as targets, for the circularity reason this design shares.
  • A preregistered falsifier, fired and reported. F-1 was frozen in Phase 1 and has now fired — against the class the field rates highest. Its specified test turned out unable to discriminate, which is itself reported rather than smoothed over. See the section above.
  • The result that follows from it, which is a negative one. Against that label, every linguistic dating metric is flat — perfect/imperfect 1.07×, participle 0.96×, aorist 0.83×, word length 1.01× — while lexical content moves fifteenfold and more. The most highly rated class of evidence in the field cannot distinguish epic from epic-plus-centuries, because the register is a target an interpolator aims at, not a symptom he leaks.
  • Provenance as a validity gate, then tier-stripping as an experiment. Evidence and interpretation are held apart at the record level, so "remove all 48 constraints resting on scholarly assertion and both brackets are identical to the year" is a sentence that can be computed. It could not be said before, because the two had never been separated in a form that could be recomputed.
  • Minimum cut as the robustness measure — and the theorem it yields. Leave-one-out cannot see redundancy; Menger's theorem can. What fell out is more general than this corpus: a cut counts the routes that realise a bound, so two independent chains raise it only when their precisions match. Robustness therefore cannot be bought with more evidence, only with evidence of matching precision — which is why three independently transmitted corpora raised nothing.
  • Backstop distance as the metric that remains useful once the cut is pinned at 1: not whether a bound survives, but how far it falls.
  • A positive control demanded of every argument from silence — and shown to bite in both directions, refusing the mokṣa constraint and admitting the writing one.
  • The pairing technique for claims about how forms behave. Counting a form against its own alternative in the same stem cancels the confound that makes raw frequencies useless: bhavati against bhavāti, not -āti against the dictionary. It converted three components of the grammar row from "no locus can settle this" to measured.

Known claims, now measured and re-runnable

  • That books 1 and 7 divinize Rāma and the core does not — long argued; here with every one of the 29 hits read and the seam the source itself names carved out.
  • That epic language is post-Vedic — standard; here decomposed into six components, three now attested to a re-runnable measurement, one shown permanently unmeasurable because no epic manuscript carries accent.
  • That the epics were composed in a pre-literate milieu — this is established Indology, not a finding of this work. What is added is the quantified form: √likh present eleven times in the core and never meaning "write", the same root at "draw" in the Mahābhārata archetype, and books only in its apparatus; plus the recognition-token control, 25 messengers and 18 abhijñāna and a signet ring where a letter would go.
  • That the Pāli canon narrates Pāṭaligāma becoming Pāṭaliputta — a famous passage. What is added is using it as an independent control on the epic argument that depends on the same transition.

Findings this work produced

  • Three scored rows the manuscripts contradict, one of them rated 5 of 5, and a fourth whose evidence table does not yield the bracket the source states beside it.
  • The mokṣa silence refuted as a dating signal by the Rāmāyaṇa's own late books — which are later by this network's attested constraints, demonstrably absorbed avatāra theology, and still score zero on the same vocabulary. It would have tightened the headline by 200 years.
  • The aorist refuted as a clock by a control that runs it: a fifth-century CE poet at the Vedic rate, twenty-five times the Rāmāyaṇa's.
  • The node capping both epics was presenting historical-chronological precision under an archaeological label — ablation shows archaeology alone supports nothing tighter than 150 BCE.
  • Corpus growth buys checking, not resolution, measured three times on three independently transmitted corpora, with the mechanism stated: precision scales logarithmically in corpus size, independent checks linearly.
  • A falsifiable claim about a tradition the network knows nothing about — DN ≤ writing ≤ Vinaya, entailed by constraints assembled to date Sanskrit epics.

Explicitly not new

The dates. Both brackets sit where the consensus already put them, and nothing here moved either by a year. Nor is the accretion model of the Mahābhārata new, nor the lateness of the Rāmāyaṇa's first and last books, nor the observation that devotional and didactic material accreted onto a martial narrative, nor the argument from Pāṭaliputra's absence. What changed is not the answers but which arguments for them can still be made — and, for one class of argument, the discovery that it cannot do the work it has been asked to do for a century.

What these results license

Four boundaries on the claims above, stated so they are not read wider than the evidence goes.

  • Still no redundancy anywhere. Every bound in the network has a minimum cut of 1, and adding an independently transmitted tradition did not change that — corroboration of an argument is not a second route to its conclusion — one constraint removed and it moves. Connecting the two epics through a shared referent did not change this, because both routes pass through the same terminus.
  • Retrodiction cannot validate this network. Held-out anchors recover a floor and never a ceiling, because the anchors are terminal: nothing in the corpus is later than the Cambodian and Gupta inscriptions, so nothing can bound them from above. That is a true description of the archive, and a real limit on what this kind of validation can show.
  • The evidence tier is uneven. Verse loci are checkable in seconds. The archaeological loci underwriting the same brackets are category references — "Mahājanapada settlement record" — not volume and page. Both are labelled attested, and that flatters the weaker half.
  • Compound length is not a dating signal. Proposed as a measurable seed for automatic stratification, it turns out to track descriptive versus narrative register: the earlier core carries ~35% more long compounds than the later frame books, driven almost entirely by one descriptive showpiece.
The dates did not move. What moved is the list of arguments that can still be made for them — and that turns out to be the more consequential list.
